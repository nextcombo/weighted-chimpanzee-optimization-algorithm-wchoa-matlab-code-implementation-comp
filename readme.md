💥1 Overview
Nowadays, a considerable number of heuristic algorithms are used to solve many complex and variable-sized problems. One of the most popular metaheuristic methods based on group intelligence is the Chimpanzee Optimization Algorithm (ChOA), inspired by the individual intelligence and sexual motivation of chimpanzees in group hunting. This paper proposes a weighted version of ChOA (WChOA) as an alternative solution to two main issues encountered in large-scale numerical optimization problems, such as low convergence speed and local optima traps for high-dimensional problems. The main difference between standard ChOA and WChOA lies in the provision of a position-weighted equation to improve convergence speed and avoid local optima. Moreover, in the context of group intelligence-based algorithms, the proposed method achieves a balance between exploration and exploitation. The WChOA method was evaluated under different conditions to demonstrate its superiority. To this end, a set of classic 30 single-peak, multimodal, and fixed-dimensional multimodal benchmark functions were employed to study the advantages and disadvantages of WChOA characteristics. Additionally, WChOA was tested on the IEEE Evolutionary Computation Conference Benchmark Functions (CECC06, 2019 competition). To further illustrate the performance of WChOA in large-scale numerical optimization and real-world problems, WChOA was studied through 13 high-dimensional optimization problems and 10 real-world optimization problems. The results showed that compared to the state-of-the-art methods in the literature such as ChOA, PSO, BBO, WOA, BH, ALO, GA, SCA, and GWO, WChOA demonstrated excellent performance in terms of convergence speed, probability of getting stuck in local minima, and exploration versus exploitation.
